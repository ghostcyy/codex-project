1. Introduction

Computational linguistics is an interdisciplinary field concerned with the statistical or rule-based modeling of natural language from a computational perspective.

Key Questions:

  • How can computers process and understand human language?
  • What are the limitations of traditional rule-based systems?
  • How has the paradigm shifted with the advent of neural networks?
Introduction

2. Historical Background

Rule-Based Systems (1950s-1980s)

Relied heavily on hand-crafted grammar rules. Brittle and unable to scale to broad coverage of natural language.

Statistical NLP (1990s-2010s)

Utilized probabilistic models (e.g., Hidden Markov Models). Better generalization but limited by feature engineering.

Background

3. Literature Review: Early Neural Nets

  • Bengio et al. (2003): A Neural Probabilistic Language Model. Introduced distributed representations for words (word embeddings).
  • Mikolov et al. (2013): Efficient Estimation of Word Representations in Vector Space (Word2Vec). Scaled up embedding generation.
  • Sutskever et al. (2014): Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks. Established the encoder-decoder architecture for translation.
Lit Review

3. Literature Review: The Transformer Era

  • Vaswani et al. (2017): Attention Is All You Need. Replaced recurrent layers with self-attention mechanisms.
  • Devlin et al. (2018): BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers for Language Understanding.
  • Brown et al. (2020): Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (GPT-3). Demonstrated the power of massive scaling.
Lit Review

4. Methodology

Our research focuses on fine-tuning pre-trained transformer models for low-resource languages.

The Approach

  1. Select a multilingual base model (e.g., mBERT, XLM-R).
  2. Compile a domain-specific corpus in the target language.
  3. Apply continued pre-training using Masked Language Modeling (MLM).
  4. Fine-tune on downstream tasks (NER, Sentiment Analysis).
Methodology

5. Data Collection

Data is the bottleneck for low-resource NLP. We utilized several strategies:

  • Web Scraping: Automated crawlers targeting local news websites and forums.
  • Crowdsourcing: Engaging native speakers for annotation and validation tasks.
  • Synthetic Data: Utilizing high-resource language models to generate parallel corpora via translation, followed by human filtering.

8. Discussion

The significant performance gains demonstrate the efficacy of continued pre-training on domain-specific, low-resource corpora.

  • Transfer learning mitigates the lack of massive annotated datasets.
  • However, the model still struggles with idiomatic expressions and code-switching, which are prevalent in the target language.
Discussion

9. Limitations

  • Dataset Bias: Our web-scraped corpus heavily over-represents formal news text, potentially reducing performance on informal social media text.
  • Computational Cost: Fine-tuning large language models requires significant GPU resources, making deployment difficult in resource-constrained environments.
  • Evaluation Scope: The study is currently limited to two downstream tasks.
Limitations

10. Future Directions

Subsequent research will focus on addressing current limitations:

Model Distillation

Compressing the fine-tuned model to run efficiently on mobile devices or edge servers.

Multimodal Expansion

Integrating acoustic and visual data to improve context understanding in spoken interactions.
